The campus problem

Student project teams fail in predictable ways, and none of them are about code.

They form on vibes

"Anyone want to build something?" in a group chat. Nobody aligns on goals, hours, roles or credit until it's too late.

Conflicts get avoided, not resolved

Two people want ML lead. One quietly resents the other for six weeks.

They die in week 3

Someone falls behind (midterms, a job, family) and goes silent rather than explain. The deadline slips and blame starts.

Privacy makes it worse

Nobody wants to tell four near-strangers "I can only give 4 hours" or "I have therapy Tuesdays." So they don't, and plans are built on fiction.

How it works

One campus hub in Discord. DM the bot and that DM is your private agent. Everything below happens through agents talking to each other, with people saying yes or no.

Project boardOwners post a one-line card. Agents browse and apply, or get scouted.
HandshakesGoals · hours · must-have skills · role fit, each answered from a private profile. Only ✅/❌ crosses.
Double opt-inBoth sides must say yes, and the owner's screening question is answered privately. Passing = "not a match this time."
Team serverThe owner closes the search; the bot builds the team its own server and DMs invites.
Kickoff meetingAgents find a slot privately and post an agenda. The meeting is recorded, transcribed, and turned into notes.
Roles & charterRoles come from skills and what people said in the meeting. Conflicts go up the resolution ladder, then everyone signs.
Tracked planTasks are scheduled around private busy days, with dependencies. A live tracker is kept current by the agents.
Every 3 daysScheduled sync: check-ins, bottlenecks, meet/async vote, hand-offs and PR reviews, 1:1s, rescue.

How agents settle conflicts

Each agent scores every possible deal with its own student's private priorities. Agents may reject and counter on their own, but only the student can accept.

Rung 1

Agents trade offers

Packages = who gets the role + perks for the other side (present the demo, first credit, first pick next milestone). Each agent concedes a little per round.

Rung 2

Neutral mediator

After 4 offers or on request: the Pareto-optimal deal that lifts the worse-off side highest. It reads both valuations and publishes only percentages.

Rung 3

Students decide

Nothing is final without both yeses. Either can walk away, and the next-best match is invited.

Work conflicts → 1:1

Changes requested on a PR? Either person books a 1:1. The two agents find a time privately and post a 15-minute agenda.

Schedule conflicts → reroute

Tasks flow around private busy days ("commitment that day, details private").

Capacity conflicts → rescue

Someone's behind: an early warning without the reason, and the teammate with slack is asked to take hours.

Privacy by design

Agents share what the protocol allows, never why. A leak scanner checks every public message against every private fact, live.

What Track 2 asks for

Conflicting constraints
Goals (startup vs paper), hours bands, must-have skills, two people wanting one role, busy days, task dependencies, overload, drop-outs.
What agents share
Structured protocol messages only: ✅/❌, fit %, offers, stated priorities, statuses. A leak scanner proves nothing private crosses.
When they ask students
Opting in · screening answers · accepting a teammate · closing the search · accepting an offer · mediator deals · signing · taking a task · approving a PR · booking a 1:1 · confirming meeting action items.
"We need this"
It replaces the two most awkward moments in group work, "who does what" and "I'm behind", with agents that do the talking.

Architecture: AI understands, rules decide

The LLM turns casual messages, meeting recordings and check-ins into structured data, and voices the practice students. Every decision is plain, tested code, so an agent can't leak a secret or invent an agreement. If the LLM is down, keyword parsing keeps the demo running.
